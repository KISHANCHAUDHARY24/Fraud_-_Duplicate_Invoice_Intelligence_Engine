## 11.6 Discussion
Draft: the model ranks anomalies well (ROC-AUC 0.89) and catches anomalies that make a single invoice look odd (off-hours 0.90, amount spikes 0.57). It is weak on relational patterns (duplicates, split purchases, threshold hugging), because each of those invoices looks normal on its own. These patterns are handled by the duplicate detector and rules R2/R5/R6, which is the justification for the hybrid design. See docs/machine-learning.md §4.7. [REWRITE IN YOUR OWN WORDS]

## 11.7 Hybrid contribution
Experiment (`scripts/evaluate_hybrid.py`): the hold-out months were replayed through the complete analysis pipeline, with an Isolation Forest trained on the earlier months only. An invoice counts as detected when it is sent to human review (MEDIUM or higher).

| Detector | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ML only | 0.417 | 0.284 | 0.338 |
| Rules + duplicate detector only | 0.380 | 0.614 | 0.470 |
| Hybrid (final) | 0.400 | 0.682 | 0.504 |

The two approaches are complementary: rules and the duplicate detector find relational patterns (bank change 1.00, duplicates 0.82), the model finds invoices that look odd on their own (off-hours 0.90). The first run of this experiment exposed a design weakness: a single strong ML or amount signal (10–15 points) never reached the review threshold of 30, so the combined system caught only 10% of off-hours anomalies although the model alone caught 90%. Two escalation floors were added (ML anomaly score ≥ 0.8, amount ≥ 6σ above the vendor average → at least MEDIUM), which raised recall from 0.443 to 0.682 **and** precision from 0.307 to 0.400. Caveat: the floors were chosen among four candidates on this same hold-out, so the improvement should be confirmed on independent data (Chapter 14). [REWRITE IN YOUR OWN WORDS]

## 11.8 Ethical considerations
Scores are indicators, not verdicts. Human review is mandatory. Explanations are provided. There is a risk of bias against legitimately unusual vendors, which is mitigated by review and configurable thresholds.
